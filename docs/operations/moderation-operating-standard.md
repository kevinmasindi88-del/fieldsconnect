# FieldsConnect — Moderation Operating Standard

**Classification:** Internal operational document; not a public user agreement.  
**Policy authority:** The public FieldsConnect [Community Guidelines](/code-of-conduct), Terms of Use and applicable law.  
**Owner:** Trust & Safety / Quality, Security and Continuous Improvement, with assigned moderation and administration responsibilities.

## 1. Objective and separation
Moderation protects community safety, participation and privacy. The public Community Guidelines state the behavioural requirements. This operating standard describes how authorised personnel implement them without creating additional obligations for members. The latest public policy prevails if this internal procedure conflicts with it.

Moderation personnel assess facts, safety, conduct and proportionate restrictions. Where a dispute may affect a paid mentorship, a moderation finding may inform responsibility, but **moderators do not execute refunds or payouts**. Financial authorisation must be independently controlled.

## 2. Receipt and triage
- Record the report reference, reporter, subject, content type, time, allegation and affected account or resource.
- Check for repeated reports of the same event; consolidate while preserving original evidence and provenance.
- Categorise reports consistently: harassment, bullying, discrimination, fraud, impersonation, privacy breach, sexual exploitation, child safety, IP/copyright, malicious content, spam, or other.
- For credible imminent physical harm or exploitation of a child, make immediate protective escalations and preserve evidence while following law-enforcement and data-protection obligations.
- Do not infer guilt from the number of reports or the prominence of the reporter.

## 3. Evidence and confidentiality
- Consider primary content, relevant contextual communication, timestamps and corroborating records lawfully accessible to the reviewer.
- Restrict access to the personnel handling the case; avoid broad disclosure of reporter identities and third-party data.
- Use the minimum necessary information and retain evidence only under a legitimate security, legal or dispute-resolution basis. Record reasons for exceptions.
- If a user reports personal, banking or child-related information, do not repeat that sensitive information in unnecessary notifications.

## 4. Fair and impartial assessment
- Distinguish lawful criticism and disagreement from targeted abuse, intimidation or discriminatory conduct.
- Evaluate context, severity, pattern, intent where reliable, victim vulnerability, credibility and likelihood of harm.
- A moderator with a personal, professional, financial or family interest in a case must disclose the conflict and have the matter reassigned or independently reviewed.
- Record the relevant Community Guidelines provisions, material evidence, reasoning and resulting decision.
- Apply comparable outcomes consistently, but do not publish automatic sanction tariffs that disregard individual circumstances.

## 5. Available outcomes
- Dismissal or no breach found.
- Guidance, informal notice, formal warning.
- Content/image/resource redaction, unpublishing or removal.
- Feature restriction or temporary protective suspension while a serious matter is reviewed.
- Time-limited suspension, which may use configured 12-day or 30-day options when proportionate.
- Permanent account termination for severe or repeated breaches.
- Preservation or lawful referral of evidence for serious threats, exploitation or fraudulent activity.

The least restrictive measure that reasonably addresses the risk is preferred. Immediate restrictions remain available where delay would create material harm.

## 6. Notices and communication
A report acknowledgment and any final notice should be understandable and not expose unnecessary private information. A reported account should be told the nature of the decision, the affected content or restriction, and available review path where reasonable and lawful. Anonymised official moderator messaging may be used, while internal actions remain individually attributable.

Do not promise a guaranteed resolution time unless a real operational service standard has been approved and implemented. Do not share investigation notes or sensitive reporter details simply because they were requested.

## 7. Review and appeals
- Record all challenge requests with the original case reference.
- For consequential decisions, route the review to a different authorised reviewer where practical.
- Reassess evidence, proportionality, conflicts and new information. Document any reversal or modification.
- Safety restrictions need not automatically pause during a review.
- Repeated malicious appeals or retaliatory reporting can be dealt with under the Guidelines, but good-faith disagreement must not itself be penalised.

## 8. Financial-dispute separation
Where mentorship termination, refund, chargeback or alleged misconduct overlaps with a payment:
1. Moderation determines the evidence-supported conduct finding (mentor, mentee, both, neither, or inconclusive).
2. The financial decision-maker applies the published checkout/refund terms and overriding statutory rights.
3. A separately authorised role executes any financial adjustment and maintains reconciliation records.
4. An appeal or correction is documented without erasing earlier action history.

Do not implement a blanket rule that a user loses statutory refund rights, or that a platform commission is always retained regardless of consumer protection law.

## 9. Governance, quality and updates
Accountability is role-based and privileges must be auditable. Cases involving children or significant privacy breaches require restricted review and appropriate escalation. Regularly sample moderation decisions for bias, unequal treatment, documentation gaps, access-control errors and time-to-resolution.

This is an internal control standard: operational rollout depends on staffing, permissions and QA. Implementation gaps remain tracked separately and are not represented as already working protections to members.
